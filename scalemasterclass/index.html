Part two

How you get
to that number.

Lead generation

The strategy doesn't change. You amplify it.

Keep doing what works

If content is working, keep going. Don't fall into the trap of pushing volume past 1 a day.

Add a second channel

Start another one, like YouTube, without dropping the ball on what already works for you.

Pay for better rooms

Higher-level networking, like the entrepreneurs' dinner club I've just joined. Make your story more premium.

Spot opportunities

Lean into markets as they open up. We've gone after Airbnb management and online coaching.

Be selective with referrals

You're now more valuable to referral partners. Be choosy about who you send work to.

Start spending on ads

Start allocating some of your extra cash to ad spend.

Bigger work

Bigger work, higher fees.

Spot new opportunities

Lean into the niches that emerge as you grow. Bigger businesses come as part of it.

Projects

Group structures and holding companies. High value, and you can bring in help.

Upsells

Management accounts, inbox management, virtual offices, holding companies.

Raise your minimum fees

You're worth more now. Put your minimum fees up for new clients.

And be more selective: qualify the prospect before you take the call.

Key concept

Whatever is stressing you out is your bottleneck.

Up to £5k, it was winning clients. From here it will keep changing. Treat the stress as the sign, and address it head on.

When you're at capacity

The waiting list is your most powerful tool.

Buys you time

A month to fix systems, or a few months to hire.

People wait

We worried they wouldn't. Everyone did.

You're in control

Anything time sensitive, you let in early, when you choose.

Less pressure

No rush to onboard everyone on time.

It also lets you raise your fees

With a queue, you can afford to be more selective.

  • Raise fees during this period and your firm naturally steps up a level.
  • Over time, higher-paying clients refer more higher-paying clients.

Key concept

Never put up with sub-par people. Ever.

Most people aren't good enough, so be quick to let them go. Bookkeepers, editors, UK staff, all of it. That's why, when I find someone good, I share them around.
