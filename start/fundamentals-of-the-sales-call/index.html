Level 7 - Sales · Video 1

Let them talk.

You can't go onto a sales call and sell someone your services. They need to decide, on the call, that they like you and trust you. The only way to help someone trust you is to listen, ask intelligent questions and answer their questions. If you talk at them, it pushes them away, creates sales resistance, and they won't particularly trust or like you.

The call, in order (tap a point to see why)

  1. Keep it simple and focused.

  2. “Why you reached out today” is their pain point. Recognise it, because that is what you link back to later in the call.

  3. These are questions that help them think the pain point out. For example: “What is your current tax planning process with your current accountant?” (ask it knowing full well they might not have one) or “Why did you want a call with me today if you already have an accountant?”

    From the connectors lesson: “What's your tax planning process with your current accountant? Do you have a pre year end call?” and “How do you communicate with your accountant currently? I assume you have a WhatsApp group?”

  4. This is your chance to talk about what you do, but be selective. Keep it brief, only the important bits, and don't go on too much.

  5. Answering their questions builds trust. And once they have worked out their pain points, it gives us the opportunity to talk about what we do.

  6. Ask this before giving the price.

  7. Price comes at the end. If you feel comfortable, give the price on the call, gauge their reaction and handle the objection there and then. If not, give a range, or send it by email afterwards. Don't put too much pressure on this at the start.

Who talks on the call

You pitch. They switch off.
