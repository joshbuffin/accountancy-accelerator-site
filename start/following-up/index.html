Level 7 - Sales · Video 8

Follow up once. Then let go.

A reminder of the lesson from Do not chase your proposals.

  1. Chasing kills leads. It is more likely to lose you the lead than win it. If someone wants to buy from you, they'll buy from you anyway.
  2. Tell them you won't keep chasing. It puts you in a stronger position because you're not desperate, and you start to see yourself as a valuable accountant, not someone needy.
  3. Spend your time on your inputs. That's lead generation: more marketing, more networking events, more social posts, better ads. Not chasing people.
  4. Tie your emotions to your inputs, not to the yeses. Don't tie them to things you can't control.
  5. They will come back. Every client has deadlines: their year end, a return, a self assessment, or the accountant they don't like messing something up.

Josh's one follow-up

Follow up once, about two weeks after sending the proposal, and only for the ones you really care about. Anchor it to the start date they gave you on the call. Calling via WhatsApp is the best way to catch them.

"I know you said your ideal onboarding date was [date], which is six weeks away now. Just checking, is that something we're working towards?"
