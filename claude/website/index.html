Build and launch your firm's website.

This prompt has Claude Code design, write, build and launch a professional website for your firm, on your own domain, without a web designer, a page builder or a monthly platform fee.

You provide the brief, your branding, your photos and videos, and the websites you like. Claude does the design, copywriting, coding, mobile optimisation and technical set-up, and walks you through the parts only you can do, such as logging into your domain provider. Once it's live, you keep editing it with Claude by typing what you want changed. No developer needed.

Overview

How it works

Claude works through ten stages and stops after each one so you can check and give feedback:

1

Plan. Claude reads your brief, studies the websites you like and writes a plan: every page, what goes on it, the design direction and the Google search terms to target.

2

Prepare assets. Your logo, photos and videos are resized and compressed for the web, and a favicon and social sharing image are created.

3

Choose a design. Claude builds 2 or 3 different homepage designs for you to choose from.

4

Build. Every page is built and written in your tone of voice, including a working contact form, booking link and legal pages.

5

Make it look good. Claude screenshots every page and refines spacing, alignment, typography and imagery with you.

6

Optimise for mobile. Every page is tested and fixed at phone and tablet sizes.

7

Tidy up. The code is cleaned up, and links, Google (SEO), speed, accessibility and analytics are checked and fixed.

8

Put it live. The site goes onto free hosting at a temporary address, and you test everything, including a real form submission.

9

Connect your domain. Claude walks you through pointing your domain at the new site, without breaking your email.

10

After launch. Google Search Console, Google Business Profile, where to update your link, and three commands for editing, checking and publishing the site from then on.

Allow a weekend, or a few evenings. Most of the time goes into your feedback on design and wording, which is time well spent.

Preparation

Before you start: things to have ready

Do Guide 1 first. You've completed Teach Claude about your firm, and you're in the Claude desktop app's Code tab with the same Firm AI folder open. Claude uses what you set up in Guide 1 for your services, pricing, ideal client and tone of voice.

Node.js will be needed. If you don't have it, Claude will walk you through installing it.

Your domain login. The login for wherever your domain name is registered (for example GoDaddy, 123-reg, Namecheap or IONOS). If you don't own a domain yet, buy one first.

Your email provider. Know who provides your email (Google Workspace, Microsoft 365, etc.). This matters when connecting the domain.

Websites you like. 3 to 5 of them, and what you like about each. Screenshots of the bits you love are even better.

Your logo, ideally as an SVG or a high-resolution PNG with a transparent background.

Your brand colours and fonts (hex codes if you have them).

Photos. Team headshots (consistent style), team and working photos. Real photos of your team beat stock photos every time.

Videos, if you have them. A short homepage clip, an about-us video, testimonial videos, or YouTube links.

Real testimonials and reviews, with permission to use the client's name.

Your firm's details for the footer and legal pages: company number, registered office, VAT number, ICO registration number and professional body details.

A booking link if you use one (for example Calendly).

# FIRM WEBSITE: BUILD AND LAUNCH BRIEF FOR CLAUDE CODE

## PART 1: INSTRUCTIONS FOR CLAUDE (do not edit this part)

I want you to design, build and launch a professional website for my accountancy practice, and set it up so I can keep updating it with you afterwards. My brief, branding, content and practical details are in Part 2. My images, logo and videos are in website/assets-inbox/ (if that folder doesn't exist yet, tell me to create it and what to put in it).

You already know my firm from CLAUDE.md and the context/ folder. Use it for services, pricing, ideal client, tone of voice and differentiators, so I don't have to repeat myself. If those files don't exist, tell me and suggest I run the firm setup prompt first, but carry on if I say so.

Work through the stages below in order. At the end of each stage, stop, explain in plain English what you've done and what I should look at, and wait for me to say "continue". I may not be technical: when I need to do something myself (create an account, log into my domain provider, approve something), walk me through it one step at a time and wait for me to confirm before moving on.

### NON-NEGOTIABLE PRINCIPLES (apply to every stage)
1. Truthful content only. Never invent testimonials, reviews, client names, statistics, awards, qualifications, team members or case studies. If I haven't given you something, use a clearly marked placeholder (for example [TESTIMONIAL NEEDED]) and add it to a missing-content list. No claims that can't be backed up ("the UK's best accountants", "guaranteed tax savings"), in line with UK advertising rules.
2. No fake people. Never use AI-generated or stock photos presented as our team or our clients. Stock or abstract imagery is fine for backgrounds if I've approved it.
3. Ask before anything goes public. Never deploy, publish or make the site live without my explicit "yes" each time. Never change DNS, domain or email settings yourself: tell me exactly what to change and where, and I'll do it.
4. Protect my email. When we connect the domain, never tell me to change or delete MX, SPF, DKIM or DMARC records, or anything else my email depends on. Only add or change the records the website needs. Before I change anything, have me screenshot or copy my current DNS records as a backup.
5. Don't lose my existing site. If I already have a website, capture its content and page addresses before replacing it, and set up redirects from old page addresses to the new ones, so links and Google rankings aren't lost.
6. Keep it simple and fast. Build a static site in plain HTML, CSS and a small amount of JavaScript: no heavy frameworks, no page builders, no paid plugins. It must be fast, secure, cheap or free to host, and easy for you to edit later.
7. Use my voice. Follow context/tone-of-voice.md and Part 2, section G. UK English throughout.
8. Respect professional rules. Use professional body names, designations and logos, and software partner badges, only as I've confirmed I'm entitled to, and flag anything I should check against my professional body's or partner programme's rules.

### STAGE 1: BRIEF, RESEARCH AND PLAN
1. Read CLAUDE.md, the context/ files and Part 2.
2. Look at every inspiration website in Part 2, section C. Visit each one if you can fetch web pages, and look at any screenshots I've saved in website/assets-inbox/inspiration/. For each, note what specifically works (layout, typography, use of colour and photography, how the homepage leads to a call to action, how services are presented). Take inspiration from patterns; never copy their wording, images or design wholesale.
3. If I have an existing website, list its pages and content, and note what to keep, rewrite or drop.
4. Write website/brief.md containing:
   - the site's goal, main call to action and target visitor (from Part 2)
   - the sitemap: every page, its purpose and its address (for example /services/bookkeeping)
   - a page-by-page content plan: the sections on each page, in order, with what each section must achieve
   - the design direction: mood, layout style, colour use, typography, photo and video style, with the reasons it suits my ideal client
   - the SEO plan: the main search terms and locations to target, and which page targets which term
   - the contact and lead capture plan (form fields, booking link, where submissions go)
   - an asset list: everything I've provided, and everything still missing
5. Ask me about any gaps one question at a time, most important first. Then summarise the plan in plain English and wait for my approval.

### STAGE 2: PREPARE THE ASSETS
1. Logo: produce the versions the site needs: full logo for the header, a version that works on dark backgrounds if needed, a square mark, a favicon set (browser tab icon, Apple touch icon) and a social sharing image (1200x630) for when the site is shared on LinkedIn, WhatsApp and so on. Prefer SVG for the logo. If I've only provided a low-quality image, tell me and ask for a better file rather than using a blurry logo.
2. Photos: resize each one to the largest size it will actually display at, convert to WebP (keeping a JPG fallback only where needed), compress them, and give them clear descriptive filenames (team-jane-smith.webp, not IMG_4432.jpg). Check headshots are consistently cropped. Tell me if any photo is too low-resolution or poorly lit to use.
3. Videos: compress each one for the web (MP4, H.264, sensible bitrate; a background hero video should ideally be under about 5 MB and 10 to 20 seconds long, muted and looping), and create a poster image for each video so something shows while it loads. If you need a tool such as ffmpeg, walk me through installing it.
4. Save processed assets in website/site/img/ and website/site/video/. Keep my originals untouched in assets-inbox.
5. Update the asset list in brief.md and show me anything missing or unusable.

### STAGE 3: DESIGN DIRECTION
1. Create 2 or 3 different design directions as working homepage mock-ups (the top of the homepage: navigation, hero section, the first two sections and a call to action), using my real logo, colours, fonts and imagery. Save them as separate HTML files I can open, and open them for me.
2. Explain the difference between them in one or two sentences each, and tell me which you'd recommend and why.
3. Once I pick one (or a mix), turn it into a design system in a single shared stylesheet: colour tokens, font sizes, spacing scale, buttons, cards, section styles, form styles, and how images and video are treated. Every page must use this system, so the whole site stays consistent and a colour change happens in one place.

### STAGE 4: BUILD ALL THE PAGES
1. Build every page in the sitemap in website/site/, with a shared header, navigation and footer on every page. Navigation: logo, the main pages, and a clear call-to-action button (for example "Book a free call") always visible.
2. Write the copy for every page using my answers in Part 2 and my context files, in my tone of voice. For each page: a clear headline that says what we do and for whom, benefit-led sections (what the client gets, not just what we do), proof (real testimonials, reviews, numbers I've confirmed), answers to the common objections and FAQs, and a call to action at the end of every page. Keep paragraphs short and scannable.
3. Contact page, and contact points across the site:
   - a contact form with the fields I've listed in Part 2, section H, including "How did you hear about us?" so we can track where leads come from
   - the form must work on a static site: set it up with a form service (Formspree, Netlify Forms or similar, whichever suits my hosting) so that submissions are emailed to me, with spam protection (a honeypot field, plus a captcha if needed), clear validation messages, and a thank-you message or page after sending
   - booking link or embedded calendar if I have one, plus click-to-call phone, click-to-email and a WhatsApp link if I use it
   - office or registered address, opening hours and expected response time
4. Legal and compliance pages and details:
   - privacy policy (UK GDPR) covering the contact form, analytics and cookies, based on the details I give in section I. Mark it as a draft for me to check, and say so clearly.
   - cookie notice and consent banner, if we use any non-essential cookies (analytics, Meta pixel): nothing non-essential loads before consent
   - complaints procedure and terms, if I've provided them
   - a footer on every page with the registered company name, company number, registered office, place of registration (for example "Registered in England and Wales"), VAT number if I want it shown, and the regulatory statement for my professional body
5. Also build: a friendly 404 "page not found" page, and redirects from my old site's page addresses (if I have one).
6. Then open the site locally for me to click through, and give me the missing-content list (placeholders still to replace).

### STAGE 5: MAKE IT LOOK GOOD (DESIGN POLISH)
1. Take screenshots of every page at desktop (1440px wide), tablet (768px) and mobile (390px) widths, for example with Puppeteer, and actually look at them.
2. Review each screenshot against this checklist and fix what you find:
   - consistent spacing between sections, and nothing cramped or floating
   - alignment on a clear grid: edges line up, and no stray off-centre elements
   - type: a clear hierarchy (one H1 per page), comfortable line lengths (roughly 60 to 75 characters), no orphaned single words on headlines where avoidable
   - colour: the brand colours used deliberately, with text contrast meeting WCAG AA
   - images: correctly cropped (no cut-off heads), consistent styles, nothing stretched or pixelated
   - buttons and links: consistent style, an obvious primary action, hover and focus states
   - the page has visual rhythm: alternate layouts and backgrounds so long pages don't feel like a wall of text
   - subtle, tasteful motion only (gentle fade-ins), respecting "reduce motion" settings
3. Show me before and after screenshots of the main changes, then ask for my feedback. Expect several rounds; make each change in the shared design system wherever possible.

### STAGE 6: OPTIMISE FOR MOBILE
Most visitors arrive from a phone (often from social media), so treat mobile as the main experience, not an afterthought:
- a working mobile menu (hamburger menu), with the call-to-action button reachable at all times
- no sideways scrolling on any page at 360px width
- body text at least 16px; buttons and links at least 44px tall and easy to tap, with space between them
- forms easy to fill in on a phone: the right keyboard for each field (email, phone), large inputs, no tiny checkboxes
- click-to-call and WhatsApp links work
- hero videos: a poster image on mobile and for data-saver settings, so the page never waits for a large video
- images sized for mobile, so phones don't download desktop-sized files
- test at 360px, 390px and 430px wide plus tablet, screenshot each page, and fix anything that breaks
Show me the mobile screenshots of every page.

### STAGE 7: TIDY THE CODE AND TECHNICAL QUALITY
1. Code tidy-up: remove unused CSS, JavaScript, images and test files; consistent formatting and indentation; clear comments where useful; one shared stylesheet and script; no inline styles scattered through pages; valid HTML.
2. Links: check every internal and external link and every image path, and fix anything broken.
3. Search engine optimisation:
   - a unique title (under about 60 characters) and meta description (under about 155 characters) for every page, targeting the terms in the SEO plan
   - one H1 per page and a logical heading structure
   - descriptive alt text on every meaningful image
   - structured data (schema.org AccountingService or LocalBusiness) with the firm name, address, phone, opening hours and links to our social profiles
   - sitemap.xml, robots.txt and canonical links
   - Open Graph and social tags, so shared links show the right title, description and image
4. Speed: lazy-load images and videos below the fold, preload the main font and hero image, set image width and height to stop the layout jumping, and keep JavaScript small. Run a Lighthouse audit (for example with npx lighthouse) on the homepage and two other pages, on mobile, and aim for 90+ in Performance, Accessibility, Best Practices and SEO. Show me the scores and fix the main issues.
5. Accessibility: form labels, keyboard navigation, visible focus states, a skip-to-content link, captions or transcripts where videos include speech, and sufficient colour contrast.
6. Analytics: add the analytics I chose in section J (only after cookie consent where required), and set up tracking for form submissions and booking clicks as conversions, so I can see how many enquiries the site generates.

### STAGE 8: PUT IT LIVE (HOSTING)
Recommend a host based on Part 2, section K. Unless I've chosen otherwise, use Netlify (free plan, simple, works well for static sites and forms). Then:
1. Walk me through creating the hosting account, one step at a time.
2. Deploy the site to a temporary address on the host (for example something.netlify.app). Ask me before deploying.
3. On the temporary address, run a full pre-launch test with me:
   - every page loads, on desktop and on my actual phone
   - send a real test enquiry through the contact form, and check it arrives in my inbox (not spam)
   - booking link, phone, email and WhatsApp links all work
   - analytics records the visit and the form conversion
   - the cookie banner works
   - the 404 page shows for a made-up address
4. Fix anything that fails, then ask me for the final go-ahead to connect my domain.

### STAGE 9: CONNECT MY DOMAIN
1. Find out where my domain is registered and where its DNS is managed (Part 2, section A). If I'm not sure, help me find out (for example with a WHOIS lookup or checking my billing emails).
2. Before anything changes: have me copy or screenshot every existing DNS record, especially the MX, SPF (TXT), DKIM and DMARC records that my email relies on.
3. Add the domain in the hosting dashboard, then tell me exactly which records to add or change at my domain provider, using the values the host shows: typically an A record or ALIAS for the root domain and a CNAME for www. Explain each record in plain English. Never ask me to touch email records.
4. Choose one main address (with or without www) and redirect the other to it.
5. Make sure HTTPS (the padlock) is switched on and that http:// redirects to https://.
6. Explain that DNS changes can take from a few minutes up to 48 hours to work everywhere. Then check the domain is live, the padlock works, both www and non-www addresses go to the right place, my old page addresses redirect properly, and email still works (send and receive a test email).
7. If my old website was on a paid platform (Wix, Squarespace, WordPress hosting), remind me not to cancel it until the new site has been live and working for a week or two, then tell me what to cancel.

### STAGE 10: AFTER LAUNCH
1. Google Search Console: walk me through verifying the domain, submitting sitemap.xml, and requesting indexing of the main pages.
2. Google Business Profile: tell me to update the website link, and check our name, address and phone match the website exactly.
3. Give me a list of everywhere else to update the link: social media bios, email signatures, LinkedIn company page, directories, my professional body's listing, software partner directories (such as the Xero advisor directory).
4. Version history: set the site up in git (and optionally GitHub, connected to the host so publishing is automatic), so that every change is saved and any mistake can be undone. Explain how this works in plain English.
5. Create Claude Code slash commands in .claude/commands/ so I never need to paste this brief again:
   - /site-edit: make a change to the website (content, design or a new page), preview it locally with desktop and mobile screenshots, and wait for my approval
   - /site-check: re-run the quality checks (broken links, mobile screenshots, Lighthouse, SEO tags, missing alt text) and report what needs fixing
   - /site-publish: show me a summary of what has changed since the last publish, ask for my explicit yes, then deploy and confirm the live site has updated
6. Write website/README.md: a plain-English guide to the site (folders, how to edit, how to publish, where the form submissions go, which accounts and logins are involved, and how to roll back a mistake). Add a short "Website" section to CLAUDE.md pointing to the commands and these principles.
7. Finish with a summary of what's live, the remaining missing-content list, and the 5 most valuable improvements to make over the next month (for example adding real testimonials, a service page for a key search term, a lead magnet download).

Your domain

Connecting your domain: what to expect

This is the part people worry about most. It's straightforward if you know what's happening:

What DNS is. Your domain name (for example myfirm.co.uk) is registered with a company like GoDaddy or 123-reg. Its "DNS records" are like signposts: one set tells the internet where your website lives, another tells it where your email goes.

What you'll change. You change only the website signposts (usually an A record and a CNAME record) to point at your new host. The email signposts (MX, SPF, DKIM and DMARC records) stay exactly as they are. Claude will tell you precisely what to add or change.

Back up first. Copy or screenshot your existing records before you change anything, so you can always put them back.

Be patient. Changes can work within minutes, but can take up to 48 hours to reach everyone. If the new site isn't showing straight away, that's normal.

Don't cancel the old site yet. If you're moving from Wix, Squarespace or similar, keep it running until the new site has been live for a week or two, then cancel.

Good to know

Lessons we learned building ours

Design for the phone first. Most visitors arrive from social media on a phone. Judge every page on your phone first.

Real faces convert. Real team photos and short real videos build far more trust than stock imagery. A simple, well-lit headshot session is one of the best investments you can make.

Compress your videos. Large hero videos are the most common reason a site feels slow. Keep them short, muted and compressed, with a still image for mobile.

Track where leads come from. Asking "How did you hear about us?" on the contact form is the easiest way to start tracking which marketing actually brings in clients.

One clear call to action. A generic "Contact us" form underperforms. A specific next step ("Book a free 15-minute call") with a promised response time works better.

Keep claims honest. Only show reviews, numbers and awards you can back up. It matters for trust, and for advertising rules.

Iterate. The design comes together in the review rounds. Don't accept the first version: tell Claude exactly what feels off, even if it's just "this section feels cramped".

Check

If something goes wrong

The domain isn't showing the new site. DNS can take up to 48 hours. Check the records match what Claude gave you exactly, then wait. Ask Claude to check the domain for you.

Email has stopped working. Stop and put your email records back from your backup straight away, then ask Claude to help work out what changed.

Form enquiries aren't arriving. Check your spam folder and the form service's dashboard, then ask Claude to run a test.

The phone shows an old version. Your phone may be showing a saved older version. Refresh the page or try a private browsing tab.

You made a change you regret. Every change is saved in version history. Ask Claude: "Undo the last change to the website and republish."
