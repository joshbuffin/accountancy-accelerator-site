The intro message.

You're not selling. You're asking for an introduction. Four parts, sent as a voice note, a call or a WhatsApp, to everyone in your phone who's even remotely valuable.

The four parts

Part 1

Make it unique to them

  • Open with something specific to that person. Never a copy-and-paste blast.
  • Their work, a hobby, something you know they've done.
  • e.g. "Saw you've been smashing it with the management company." / "I know you've been doing loads of running."

Part 2

Compliment their network

  • Tell them you know they're really well connected in their area, industry or space.
  • In a group chat: "I know people in here are really well connected."

Part 3

Tell a personal story

  • Why you're making the jump. Make it about life, not business.
  • e.g. "My kids are growing up way too fast. I want to work from home and watch them grow up."
  • e.g. "We've just had a baby and I've realised how much I want to be around for it."
  • Use your real reason. That's what makes people care.

Part 4

Ask for an intro, not work

  • "Does anyone come to mind you could introduce me to, who might be a good contact for me?"
  • No pitch. No price. No "do you need an accountant?"

Why it works

Attention

The compliment earns it

  • Compliment first, then story, and people are far more likely to care about what you say next.

Connection

The story makes them want to help

  • The people best placed to help are often 10+ years ahead of you. Their kids are 10 or 16 now. They remember how fast it went.
  • They relate to you, so they want to help. You're appealing to feelings, not selling.

Low pressure

An intro is an easy yes

  • You're asking for a favour, not money. There's nothing to object to.

Two for one

It does two jobs at once

  • You get access to their network.
  • They now know you're an accountant. If they need one, they'll come to you anyway.

Baby step selling

A step of trust, not a sale

  • Break "stranger to client" into as many small steps as possible. All you want here is for them to like and trust you a little. Let it progress from there.

The numbers

Play the odds

  • Most people will just say "amazing, good for you". That's fine.

1 in 10might open six doors for you

Where to use it

Everywhere

Same structure, every channel

  • Phone and WhatsApp contacts: list them in a spreadsheet, grade each out of 5, add notes after every conversation.
  • Group chats and paid networks: add value first (reply, link people together, share articles), then post it.
  • Niche pockets: lean into why you know their world.
  • Face to face (padel, the gym, coworking, the school gates): ask "what do you do for work?", wait for them to ask you back, then tell the story.
  • Voice notes and calls beat text when it suits the person.

Don't

What not to say

  • "Hey guys, here's what I've just launched."
  • "My service is better than the firm down the road."
  • Anything that sounds like a pitch.
