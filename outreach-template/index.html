The mindset

Baby step selling.

Break "stranger to client" into as many small steps as possible. This message is just one small step.

All you want

For them to like and trust you a little.

Let it progress from there.

Job one

Access to their network.

They know another 30 people. You don't.

Job two

They now know you're an accountant.

If they need one, they'll come to you anyway.

Who to message

Everyone in your phone.

Anyone even remotely valuable: they've had a business, or they know people. Even someone you worked with in a pub two years ago. Just not anyone who'd get you in trouble at work.

1

Go through every contact

Phone and WhatsApp.

2

Grade each one out of 5.

3

In protected, high-energy time, like 10am to 2pm on your best days.

4

So you remember what each person said.

Format

Voice note or call beats text.

When it suits the person, send a voice note or just ring them. The more personal, the better.

The script, part by part

Four parts. One favour.

1

Make it personal

"Hi [Name], hope you're well. [Something specific to them: their work, a hobby, or "it's been a while!"]"

Why

Never a copy-and-paste blast. One line that only fits them.

2

Compliment their network

"I wanted to drop you a message because I know you're really well connected in [area / industry / space]."

Why

Now they want to prove you right. "You think I'm a useful guy? Let me show you."

3

Tell your story

"I'm in the process of handing in my notice and making the jump to running my own accounting firm, because [your real reason]."

My kids are growing up too fast. I want to work from home and watch them grow up.We've got a baby on the way.I got married and want more control over my life.We've just bought a house.
Why

A compliment and a story make them care about what you say next. The people best placed to help are often 10+ years ahead of you. They remember how fast it went.

4

Ask for an intro, not work

"I'm just wondering, is there anyone in your network you could introduce me to who might need an accountant, or anyone who's mentioned it to you recently?"

Optional: "I know if it came from you, it would carry a lot of weight, because I know how well respected you are."

Why

You're asking for a favour, not money. There's nothing to object to.

If they ask about a specialism

You don't need to niche publicly.

"To be honest, for most of the small businesses I work with, the tax isn't overly complex. What they really need is someone proactive and good at communicating."

Same structure, every channel

Other versions.

Group chat or paid community

Add value first. Then post.

Hey guys, I know people in here are really well connected. I'm making the move from full-time employment to running my own accounting practice, because [your story]. Does anyone have someone they could introduce me to who might be a good contact for me?

Before you post: reply to people, link people together, share useful articles. In paid networks, going to the in-person events and following up beats messaging the group.

Someone 10 years ahead of you

Ask for advice, not work.

Hi [Name], I know you're doing really well in [their space]. I'm just starting my own business and I'd love to borrow a few minutes of your time to pick your brain on [marketing / chasing clients / your question]. Would you be up for a quick call or a coffee?

On the call: go in to genuinely get value. By the end they often offer intros themselves: "That's me ten years ago. I know someone who can help."

Door to door

Find the owner. Keep it short.

Hi, are you the owner? Really sorry to bother you, I know you're busy. I've finally made the jump and started my own accounting firm, and I'm looking for introductions locally because I live just down the road. Do you know anyone who's looking for an accountant? I'll get out of your way. Here's my card.

Then: try to get their WhatsApp number or email, and write a note straight after.

Everyday life

Padel, sauna, gym, co-working, school gates.

"How's your week looking?" "What do you do for work?" [Wait for them to ask you back.] "I'm actually making the jump to running my own accounting firm, because [your story]."

No pitch. Swap WhatsApp numbers, not business cards. Your input is how many people you ask.

The odds

You're looking for one golden intro.

Illustrative

Most won't reply.
Most who do say "Amazing, good for you."
One opens several doors.

One or two super-referrers is all you need.

Don't

Anything that sounds like a pitch.

"Hey guys, here's what I've just launched."

"My service is better than the firm down the road."

"Do you need an accountant?" as your opener.
