Section 1

Year-end journal adjustments

Quick wins you can usually put through at year-end.

Mileage (own car used for business)

Use HMRC's Approved Mileage Allowance Payments (AMAP):

VehicleRate per business mile
Cars and vans: first 10,000 miles in the tax year55p
Cars and vans: every mile after 10,00025p
Motorcycles24p
Bicycles20p
Each colleague carried as a passenger on the same business trip5p

Keep a mileage log showing the date, purpose, from/to and miles travelled.

AMAP already covers fuel, insurance and wear and tear, so do not also claim actual running costs.

Example: 12,000 business miles in a car = (10,000 × 55p) + (2,000 × 25p) = £5,500 + £500 = £6,000 claim.

Reviewing an earlier year? The 55p rate applies from 06/04/2026. Up to 05/04/2026 the first 10,000 miles were 45p.

Company cars are different. Use HMRC's quarterly Advisory Fuel Rates or Advisory Electricity Rates instead of AMAP.

Working from home (directors or employees)

Two straightforward options:

1

Flat rate. The company can pay £6 per week (£312 per year) tax-free with no receipts if the employee works regularly from home. This is simple and needs very little admin.

2

Actual costs (apportionment). If costs are higher than £6 per week, reimburse the extra household costs that come from homeworking (such as heat, light, metered water and business calls) using a reasonable split based on rooms and time. Keep the bills and calculations, and only claim the additional cost.

Changed from 06/04/2026: employees can no longer claim the £6 a week relief themselves through their tax return. The tax-free payment now has to come from the employer, so put it through the company.

Section 2

What to do with excess profits

ISAs

Allowance: £20,000 per adult each tax year. This can be split across Cash, Stocks and Shares, and Innovative Finance ISAs.

Lifetime ISA: up to £4,000 a year, which counts towards the £20,000 allowance. Open it before 40 and pay in until 50; the government adds a 25% bonus (up to £1,000 a year).

Since April 2024 you can pay into more than one ISA of the same type in a single tax year, if the provider allows it.

Returns and interest inside an ISA are tax-free, so future dividends or interest will not eat into the client's other allowances.

Pensions

Company (employer) contributions are usually deductible for Corporation Tax if they are wholly and exclusively for the purposes of the business.

Annual allowance: generally £60,000 (can be tapered for high incomes). You can carry forward three years of unused allowance.

The Lifetime Allowance has been abolished. Tax-free lump sums are now limited by the Lump Sum Allowance, normally £268,275, and the Lump Sum and Death Benefit Allowance, normally £1,073,100.

For owner-managed companies, employer pension contributions are often a very tax-efficient way to take profits out, within the allowance rules.

The employer pays Class 1A National Insurance (15%) on the benefit.

Capital allowances: new, unused zero-emission cars qualify for a 100% first-year allowance if bought before 01/04/2027. EV charge points qualify too.

Salary sacrifice for electric cars can work very well because the benefit in kind is so low compared with petrol or diesel.

Qualifying capital expenditure (non-car equipment)

Annual Investment Allowance (AIA): claim 100% up to £1 million on most plant and machinery.

Full expensing (companies only): 100% for new main-rate plant and machinery and 50% for new special-rate assets. Cars are excluded. Assets must be new and not for leasing.

40% first-year allowance: for new main-rate plant and machinery bought on or after 01/01/2026, including assets bought to lease to others (which full expensing does not cover). Not cars.

Writing-down allowances: the main pool rate fell from 18% to 14% from April 2026. The special rate pool stays at 6%.

Time significant purchases before year-end to bring the relief forward, where cash flow allows.

Section 4

Payment efficiencies

Personal allowance for salary

For most director-owners in England, Wales and Northern Ireland, a common approach is to set gross salary at the Personal Allowance (£12,570) and take the rest as dividends.

The best salary depends on Employment Allowance eligibility and National Insurance thresholds:

Employer National Insurance: 15% on pay above £5,000 a year.

Employment Allowance: up to £10,500 off the employer NI bill. Single-director companies with no other employees cannot claim it.

Dividends

£500 tax-free dividend allowance in 2026/27.

Dividends above this are taxed at 10.75% (basic rate), 35.75% (higher rate) and 39.35% (additional rate). The basic and higher rates went up by 2 points from 06/04/2026.

Savings allowance

Personal Savings Allowance: £1,000 for basic-rate taxpayers, £500 for higher-rate taxpayers and £0 for additional-rate taxpayers.

Clients with low earnings may also get the £5,000 starting rate for savings (available in full if their other taxable income is below £17,570).

Coming up: from 06/04/2027 the tax rates on savings income are due to rise by 2 points, to 22%, 42% and 47%.

Trivial benefits

Give small, non-cash benefits, such as a £40 gift card, that are not in return for work performed.

Per-item cap: £50.

Close-company directors: annual cap of £300 in total per director.

Keep receipts and a simple log.

Staff entertainment

The annual function exemption lets you provide staff events tax-free if:

the event is annual (such as a Christmas party)

it is open to all staff, or all staff at one location

the cost is no more than £150 per head, including VAT, travel and accommodation

Go over £150 per head by even £1 and the whole event becomes taxable. You can split the £150 across several events, as long as the combined total per head stays within £150.

Section 5

Structuring

Employing a spouse

This is legitimate if it is for real work at a commercial rate. Put them on PAYE, pay at or above the National Minimum Wage (£12.71 an hour for those aged 21 and over from 01/04/2026) unless a specific exemption applies, keep timesheets or a job description, and pay them through payroll like any other employee.

HMRC looks for evidence that the pay is for genuine work.

Employing children

Follow the child employment rules on minimum age, permits and hours. Pay a reasonable market rate through payroll. Under-13s generally cannot be employed, and strict working-hours rules apply until school-leaving age.

Holding companies

Advantages

Receive most intra-group dividends tax-exempt.

Ring-fence cash and assets.

Potentially sell a trading subsidiary free of Corporation Tax on the gain under the Substantial Shareholding Exemption, if the conditions are met.

Points to watch

Extra administration.

You need transfer-pricing or management-charge evidence.

Check the Substantial Shareholding Exemption conditions before relying on it: the holding company must have held at least 10% of the shares for a continuous 12 months within the 6 years before the sale, and the subsidiary must be a trading company.

Figures checked on gov.uk on 27/11/2026 for the 2026/27 tax year. Rates change every Budget, so check the current figure before you advise a client.
